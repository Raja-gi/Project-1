Can a landlord change the locks on a tenant?

Not while the tenant still lives there. Locks can be changed once possession has been given back, either because the tenant has left or because a county court bailiff or High Court Enforcement Officer has carried out a possession order. Changing them earlier is an illegal eviction.

FAQs

Questions from landlords and agents

Anything else? Call 07958 033395 and ask.

Can you attend an eviction?+

Yes. We change the locks on the day, alongside your court enforcement officer or bailiff, once they have taken possession.

Do you deal with tenants directly?+

Yes, if you want us to. We arrange access and let you know when the job is done.

Can you invoice my agency?+

Yes. Invoices carry your reference and the property address. Ask us about paying on account.

Do you cover HMOs and blocks?+

Yes. Room door locks, communal doors and keyed-alike or suited key systems.

What is the difference between a bailiff and an HCEO?+

County court bailiffs enforce a warrant of possession. High Court Enforcement Officers enforce a writ of possession, once the case has been moved to the High Court. Either way, we change the locks once they have taken possession.

Do you work with letting agents?+

Yes. We can take instructions from your agent, arrange access with the tenant and invoice the agency with your reference.

Do bedroom doors in a shared house need special locks?+

Council HMO standards usually ask for room locks that open from the inside without a key. We fit thumb-turn locks and key each room differently.
